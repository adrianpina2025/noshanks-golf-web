Scotland or Ireland: Choosing the Right Links Trip

The choice is less about which destination is better and more about which version of the trip fits your group.

August 24, 20266 min readNo Shanks Journal

Choose the rhythm of the trip

Scotland can feel concentrated and historic, especially when an itinerary is built around one or two neighboring regions. Ireland often rewards a road-trip mindset, with coastal drives and towns becoming part of the experience.

A group that wants to unpack once and focus relentlessly on golf may prefer a compact Scottish base. A group that enjoys movement, scenery and evenings in different towns may lean toward Ireland.

Be honest about transportation

Links trips look simple on a map. In practice, small roads, weather and post-round fatigue can make travel time meaningful. Build the itinerary around realistic transfers rather than the maximum number of courses that can be connected on paper.

A dedicated driver or operator can materially improve the experience because every player can enjoy the day without managing navigation, parking or the next departure time.

Decide what the evenings should feel like

The best itinerary protects the social part of the trip. Some groups want formal dinners and destination hotels. Others want a lively town, a relaxed meal and somewhere to revisit the day's decisions.

Ask the group early. The answer influences where to stay and how aggressively to schedule the golf.

Leave room for the weather

Wind and rain are not interruptions to links golf; they are part of its character. Pack layers, protect electronics and avoid building a schedule with no recovery time. A flexible afternoon or lighter travel day can save the entire itinerary when conditions shift.

Choose Scotland for a concentrated, tradition-rich itinerary; choose Ireland for a links journey where the roads, towns and evenings carry equal weight.
