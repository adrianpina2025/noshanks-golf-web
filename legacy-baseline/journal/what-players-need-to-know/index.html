Player Experience

What Players Need to Know—and When

Players do not want to manage the trip. They want confidence that the details are handled and the competition is current.

August 13, 20265 min readNo Shanks Journal

At invitation: make the decision easy

A player should immediately understand the dates, destination, expected budget, number of rounds and level of commitment. A clear invitation produces better responses than a long thread of partial information.

Before travel: remove the predictable questions

Arrival instructions, transportation, accommodations, rooming, packing notes, payment status and the first meeting point should be visible without contacting the organizer. Reminders should arrive close enough to the action to be useful.

Before each round: show today's experience

Players need the tee time, course, transportation departure, pairing, tees, game, handicap, weather and caddie information. That daily view should be concise enough to scan while walking out of the hotel.

During the round: keep competition effortless

The player may enter scores, but does not need to calculate the consequences. Live standings, contribution factors, projected winnings and secured results should update from the scoring rules already attached to the event.

Afterward: preserve more than a total

Final results matter, but so do the photographs, pairings, courses, weather and stories around them. A permanent record lets the group relive the experience and gives the next organizer a head start.

The best player experience asks for very little and answers questions before they become interruptions.
