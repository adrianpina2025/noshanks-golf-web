How Clubs Can Host Groups Without Holding Tee Times Blind

Group business is valuable, but unclear participant counts and scattered organizer communication create avoidable inventory risk.

August 9, 20266 min readNo Shanks Journal

Start with a shared event record

The organizer, operator and club should be working from the same dates, participant count, tee-time requirements, format and service expectations. When those details are scattered across emails, the club has no reliable picture of the group's true status.

Connect tee-time blocks to commitment

No Shanks allows the organizer and club to connect multiple tee times to confirmed participants, required deposits, payment progress and release dates. The club can see whether the event is strengthening or weakening before inventory becomes difficult to recover.

That visibility helps the club protect the group experience without over-reserving tee times that may later be cancelled.

Collect the information the operation actually needs

Participant names, handicaps, pairings, caddie needs, dietary notes, arrival times, tournament rules and billing responsibility should reach the correct club team in a structured format. Each department should not need to ask the organizer for its own version.

Extend the club's reputation beyond the property

A white-label experience lets the club present schedules, event information, live standings, photographs and recaps under its own identity. The level of organization becomes part of the service members and guests associate with the club.

Better commitment data lets a club hold the right inventory, communicate sooner and deliver a more confident group experience.
