Nassau, Skins or Match Play? Choosing the Right Game

A good game creates interest without slowing the round or turning the organizer into an accountant.

August 21, 20265 min readNo Shanks Journal

Nassau: familiar and balanced

Nassau divides the round into front nine, back nine and overall matches. It gives a side that starts poorly a fresh contest at the turn and keeps the final holes meaningful.

It works well for pairs, mixed handicaps and groups that want structure without a complicated rules briefing.

Skins: every hole can matter

Skins rewards winning a hole outright. Ties carry the value forward, which can make an ordinary hole suddenly decisive. It is easy to understand and works especially well when the group wants one shared competition alongside other matches.

Set the value, carry rules and handicap treatment before the round begins.

Match Play: a clear head-to-head contest

Match Play makes every hole its own contest. A difficult hole does not carry forward on the scorecard, so players can reset quickly and every pairing always knows where it stands.

It is a strong choice for team events, pairings and groups that want a simple live result without translating strokes into another spreadsheet.

Best Ball and Stableford

Best Ball is forgiving and familiar: the best score from each side counts. Stableford rewards scoring opportunities and keeps one difficult hole from ending a player's day.

For multi-round trips, vary the supported formats. A familiar opening game can settle the group before a team-based or points-focused format later in the itinerary.

Use handicaps consistently

The game only feels fair when everyone trusts how strokes are applied. Confirm the handicap source, allowance and tees in advance. Then let the platform calculate the live position while the group concentrates on the next shot.

Choose the simplest supported format that creates the kind of competition your group enjoys, then set it once and make the standings visible to everyone.
